Argentina won the World Cup on Sunday, beating France on penalties after a dramatic final that finished 3-3 following extra time. The match, played in front of more than 88,000 fans, is being hailed as one of the greatest finals in the tournament's history. Lionel Messi scored twice and converted his spot kick in the shootout, capping a career-long quest to lift the trophy. France's Kylian Mbappe completed a hat-trick, becoming only the second player to score three goals in a World Cup final. Argentina led 2-0 at halftime and looked comfortable before Mbappe struck twice in 97 seconds late in the second half to force extra time. Messi restored Argentina's lead in extra time, but Mbappe equalized again from the penalty spot. In the shootout, Argentina's goalkeeper made a crucial save to swing the tie. It is Argentina's third World Cup title and its first since 1986. Thousands of fans poured into the streets of Buenos Aires to celebrate through the night. Messi, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, was named the tournament's best player. The 35-year-old had said the final would be his last World Cup match. France, the defending champions, fell short in their bid to retain the title. The two teams traded the lead repeatedly in a finale that captivated a global audience.

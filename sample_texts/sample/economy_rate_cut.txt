The Federal Reserve cut its benchmark interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point on Wednesday, its first reduction in more than a year, citing cooling inflation and a softening job market. The move lowers the central bank's key rate to a range of 4.5 to 4.75 percent. Stock markets rose modestly after the decision, while the dollar weakened against major currencies. Fed officials signaled that further cuts could follow if inflation continues to ease toward their 2 percent target. "We are encouraged by the progress on inflation, but our work is not done," the Fed chair told reporters. Inflation has fallen sharply from its peak two years ago, when prices were rising at the fastest pace in four decades. The labor market, while still solid, has shown signs of slowing, with monthly job gains smaller than a year earlier. Lower rates tend to reduce borrowing costs for mortgages, car loans and credit cards. The decision was approved by a near-unanimous vote of the policy committee. Some economists had urged a larger cut, arguing the central bank risks keeping policy too tight for too long. Others warned that cutting too quickly could reignite inflation. The Fed said future decisions would depend on incoming economic data. Markets are now pricing in additional cuts over the next year. The chair declined to commit to a specific path for rates.

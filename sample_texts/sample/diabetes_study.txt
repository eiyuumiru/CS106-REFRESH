A large international study has found that a common, inexpensive diabetes drug may significantly lower the risk of developing serious heart disease, researchers reported Wednesday. The trial followed more than 10,000 patients over five years and was published in a leading medical journal. Patients taking the drug were 18 percent less likely to suffer a heart attack or stroke than those given a placebo. The findings could change how doctors treat millions of people with type 2 diabetes worldwide. "This is one of the most important results we have seen in this field in a decade," said the study's lead author, a cardiologist at a university hospital. The drug, already widely prescribed to control blood sugar, costs only a few dollars a month in many countries. Researchers said the protective effect appeared regardless of how well patients controlled their blood sugar, suggesting the benefit comes through a separate mechanism. Side effects were generally mild and similar to those seen with existing treatments. The study was funded by a government health agency, not a pharmaceutical company. Independent experts welcomed the results but cautioned that the findings should be confirmed in additional trials. Heart disease is the leading cause of death among people with diabetes. The research team plans to investigate whether the drug offers similar protection to people without diabetes. Guidelines committees are expected to review the data in the coming months.

A leading semiconductor maker on Monday unveiled what it called the world's most advanced artificial intelligence chip, claiming a threefold jump in performance over its previous generation. The company said the new processor packs more than 200 billion transistors and is built on a cutting-edge two-nanometer manufacturing process. Shares of the firm rose nearly 8 percent in early trading after the announcement. The chip is aimed at data centers training large AI models, a market that has exploded over the past two years. "This is the engine that will power the next wave of artificial intelligence," the company's chief executive said at a packed product event. Several major cloud providers have already placed orders, the company said, with shipments expected to begin in the third quarter. Analysts said the launch widens the company's lead over rivals scrambling to catch up in the lucrative AI hardware market. The new chip also promises to cut energy use per computation by roughly 40 percent, addressing growing concern over the power demands of AI. Data centers running such chips consume enormous amounts of electricity, straining local grids. The company said it worked with partners to design liquid-cooling systems for the processors. Demand for AI chips has far outstripped supply, lifting the firm's valuation past 3 trillion dollars this year. Some experts warned that the breakneck pace of investment could cool if AI applications fail to deliver promised returns. The company plans to detail a road map for future chips at a conference next month.

A fast-moving wildfire forced thousands of residents to flee their homes in Southern California on Thursday as hot winds pushed the flames toward populated foothill communities. The blaze, which started Wednesday night, had burned more than 12,000 acres by Thursday afternoon and was zero percent contained, fire officials said. At least 8,000 people were under mandatory evacuation orders, and several schools were closed. No deaths have been reported, but three firefighters suffered minor injuries battling the flames. Governor's office declared a state of emergency for the affected county, freeing up additional resources. More than 1,500 firefighters, backed by aircraft dropping water and retardant, were working to protect homes. Gusts of up to 50 miles per hour and humidity below 10 percent created what forecasters called critical fire weather. "Conditions are about as dangerous as they get," a fire department spokesman said at an evening briefing. Power was shut off to tens of thousands of customers as a precaution against downed lines sparking new fires. Evacuees were directed to shelters set up at community centers and a local fairground. Officials said the cause of the fire was under investigation. The region has endured a prolonged drought that has left brush and grass tinder-dry. Crews hoped that winds would ease over the weekend, giving them a chance to build containment lines.
